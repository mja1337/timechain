# First-run UX review — 10 October 2026

## Assessment

Timechain's strongest idea is learning through the consequences of running a mining operation against recorded history. BTC production, spendable cash and custody are distinct resources, which gives the game useful decisions beyond watching a price chart. The warm spare-room scene, expanding valley, orange controls and IRC friend give the simulation a recognisable identity. Preserve those strengths.

The first-run experience is functional and educational, but more demanding than inviting. Before the player has much experience of mining, it introduces UTXOs, randomness, temporary records, a key-holder oath, signers, backups, wallet policy and payout destinations. The ten-step tutorial is chiefly a custody lesson. It explains those actions well individually, but delays the operating loop promised by “Start your laptop”. This is an expert UX assessment from a browser walkthrough, not evidence from novice playtesting.

## Implemented in Beta 1.4

- Separated the online-wallet warning and backup action with sufficient space for the tutorial highlight; increased the action's height and made the warning easier to read.
- Corrected the responsive cascade that placed a narrow floating coach over single-column pages. Phone and tablet guidance now spans the page, with bottom scrolling clearance and target repositioning on resize.
- Kept opening actions visible while reading the long introduction and wallet explanation; allowed them to wrap on phones.
- Made the paper checkpoint explicitly simulated. Players no longer have to infer whether the game requires physical paper or claim they have written something outside the game.
- Named the phone's icon-only Add a machine control for assistive technology. Anchored the briefing's Why control to its card and reserved space beside its heading.
- Displayed the bank-box fee on the tutorial action, including that one monthly fee covers the box's contents.
- Corrected misleading pre-market guidance: online BTC is not sellable in 2009; early unpaid bills have no grace period; the forecast shows the actual cash shortfall; recovery guidance routes to machines before a market exists.
- Kept exact buy/sell amounts through quote cancellation and ticket repaints, capped to current funds. A different run receives fresh defaults.
- Removed duplicated BTC units in the custody explanation. Updated Method's obsolete room-by-room tour description and the tutorial's reference to the former footer.
- Preserved the simulation, save format and action handlers. Both the tutorial shortcut and the highlighted page button remain intentional routes to the same action; retaining the shortcut prevents a hidden or collapsed page control from blocking the lesson.

## Recommended next design decisions

1. **Keep custody first, with a clearer payoff.** The project owner chose to retain the custody-first sequence. Introduce it as protecting the rewards the laptop will earn, and make each completed step visibly improve the player's setup. Preserve the hands-on sequence and reduce competing explanations rather than moving custody into a later lesson.
2. **Reduce simultaneous explanations.** Custody currently presents workshop choices, a five-step setup, wallet metrics, recovery locations, a threat lab and multiple maps on one page, alongside the ten-step coach. Keep the active setup near the top and progressively disclose the rest. Avoid putting another checklist next to the player's current checklist.
3. **Make the first bill a compact objective.** “Plan your first bill” opens Finance, where scores, financing exposure and staff compete with the bill. Lead with amount, due date, cash remaining and one next action; let players expand the rest.
4. **Make discovery on the land map optional.** The 3D valley provides an attractive sense of growth, but its crowded property labels and distant unlocks are less useful to a new player than the laptop room. Consider opening early campaigns inside the room, with the valley as an explicit exploration action.
5. **Separate learning rewards from assessments of failure.** The new operation quickly shows an “AT RISK” score and incomplete reserve metrics, even while cash covers bills. Explain that these are unfinished progression measures, or delay prominent grading until the player has completed a month.
6. **Test with new players.** Ask several people unfamiliar with mining to reach the first bill without help. Watch for whether they can explain cash versus BTC, find the pause control, understand the next action, and distinguish the simulated paper exercise from real wallet practice. Track where they stop reading or leave, rather than relying only on completion.

## Verification and scope

Browser walkthrough: default Standard start, 3 February 2009; full dice route including keyboard input; simulated paper handling; every custody tutorial action through Start the clock; actual page backup control; automatic signer delivery and pause; reserve payout choice; return to room; land-to-room view; empty early shop; Learn; first-bill Finance route; reload; a second fresh run using Generate the key for me.

Responsive checks: desktop 1440×900, tablet 900×720 and phone 390×844, plus the initial embedded browser size. At the phone and tablet backup step, the page had no horizontal overflow and the highlighted button was fully above the tutorial card. Opening actions remained visible while the introduction was scrolled. Normal viewport restored afterward.

Market browser regression: synthetic 29 January 2013 run, seed 140010. Cancelled and confirmed a 1 BTC Mt. Gox sale; cancelled and confirmed a combined sale of 5 hot BTC; confirmed balances and two corresponding ledger entries; checked that a frozen Bitfinex balance could not be sold; reloaded and reopened Treasury without console errors. The exact amount remained 1 BTC after cancellation. Cash rose from $1,000 to $1,019 and then $1,116 (rounded UI values), while Mt. Gox fell from 10 to 9 BTC and hot BTC fell from 5 to 0.

All four required suites passed: project structure, UI contracts (including 896 randomised tutorial runs), historical-data/syntax checks and 248 engine behaviour rules. Added rendered-output assertions for pre-market versus trading-era bill guidance. `git diff --check` passed.

No browser JavaScript errors were observed in the reviewed flow. A warning about multiple Three.js instances remains; it did not prevent either view from rendering. The complete late-game campaign, real mobile devices, screen-reader operation, and a pending-bill rescue during an exchange sale were not exercised in this review. No transaction execution code was changed.
